Fuente: [Canvas](https://maristasperu.instructure.com/courses/24296/pages/session-2-iiit-celebrities). Copia consultada el 26 de octubre de 2026.

LO : Students describe the life of celebrities

Develop the following activity to review 

 Warm-Up Game: "Two Truths & A Time-Travel Lie"

Goal: Activate prior knowledge, get moving, and demonstrate how grammar changes our perspective on time.

How to Play (Step-by-Step)

1 Set the Detective Mission

1 Minute

The class: "Detectives, someone messed with the timeline! You are going to get three statements about what happened earlier today. Two are true, and one is a Time-Travel Lie (Past Perfect error). Your job is to spot the lie!"

2 Physical Voting (Movement)

2 Minutes

Read three quick statements aloud. You should vote physically with your body:

- Action A: Stand up if statement 1 is a lie. 
- Action B: Touch your nose if statement 2 is the lie. 
- Action C: Sit under your desk / squat if statement 3 is the lie.  

3 The TOK Discussion Spark

2 Minutes

Ask the "Lead Detective" who guessed correctly: "How did you KNOW that event happened first? What word was your clue?"

Example Game Rounds

Round 1: Spot the Sequence (Past Simple vs. Past Perfect)

- 1. "Before school started, I had eaten breakfast."  
- 2. "When the bell rang, I already sat down."  
- 3. "By the time class began, I had opened my notebook."   

Teacher Fix: "Why was _______ wrong? Because ____________________________________________________'"

Round 2: Spot the Focus (Past Passive Voice)

- 1. "The teacher opened the door." (Active) 
- 2."The whiteboard was erased before class." (Passive) 
- 3. "The mystery cookie was ate by a ninja."   

Teacher Fix: "Why did your detective ears tingle at ________? Passive voice needs ___________________________________'"

TOK Connection Question to transition into the main lesson:

"When we say 'The cookie was eaten' instead of 'Leo ate the cookie', what are we trying to hide? Does changing the words change who gets in trouble?
