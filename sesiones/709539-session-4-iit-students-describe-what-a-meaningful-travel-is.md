IB English SL: Travel Blogging Assessment Rubric

  |  

Criterion

   |  

Excellent (7–8 Marks)

   |  

Proficient (5–6 Marks)

   |  

Developing (3–4 Marks)

   |  

Emerging (1–2 Marks)

  |  

Criterion A: Language

• Grammar & syntax

• Vocabulary variety

• Tone & register style

   |  

• Vocabulary is sophisticated, varied, and highly appropriate for a travel blog.

• Grammar and sentence structures are complex and virtually error-free.

• Tone is perfectly consistent (e.g., engaging, reflective, or persuasive).

   |  

• Vocabulary is varied and appropriate, with minor repetitions.

• Grammar is generally accurate; minor errors do not interfere with meaning.

• Tone is appropriate for a blog, though it may falter slightly in places.

   |  

• Vocabulary is basic and repetitive; relies on simple descriptions.

• Frequent grammatical errors occasionally obscure the meaning of sentences.

• Tone is inconsistent (e.g., shifts between too formal and overly casual).

   |  

• Vocabulary is limited or inappropriate for the task.

• Persistent grammatical errors make the text difficult to understand.

• Tone is flat, inappropriate, or entirely missing.

  |  

Criterion B: Message

• Development of ideas

• Coherence & structure

• Relevance to prompt

   |  

• Ideas are deeply developed, insightful, and highly relevant to the travel theme.

• The piece flows logically with seamless transitions between paragraphs.

• Arguments or descriptions are compelling and thoroughly supported.

   |  

• Ideas are clearly stated and relevant, with good development.

• The structure is logical, using standard paragraphs and clear transitions.

• Details are provided, though some points could be expanded further.

   |  

• Ideas are superficial or only partially address the prompt.

• Structure is disjointed; formatting (like subheadings) might feel forced.

• Content lacks detail or relies on heavy generalization.

   |  

• Ideas are irrelevant, confusing, or repetitive.

• The piece lacks an organized structure or logical progression.

• Minimal effort is shown in developing the narrative or argument.

  |  

Criterion C: Conceptual Understanding

• Text-type conventions

• Audience awareness

• Digital formatting elements

   |  

• Perfect command of travel blog conventions (catchy title, subheadings, bullet points, callouts).

• Clear awareness of the target audience; the text actively connects with readers.

• Effective integration of digital features (links, placeholders for photos).

   |  

• Most travel blog conventions are present and used correctly.

• Good awareness of the audience; attempts to engage the reader directly.

• Uses basic blog formatting (e.g., a title and headings) effectively.

   |  

• Only a few blog conventions are used; reads more like a standard school essay.

• Weak sense of audience; tone feels passive rather than engaging.

• Formatting is neglected or limited to just a basic title.

   |  

• Text-type conventions are ignored entirely.

• No awareness of the audience or the purpose of a blog.

• Presented as a continuous block of text with no formatting elements.

  |
