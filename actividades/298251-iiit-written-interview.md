1. Open-Ended Question Examples (DP Level)

Open-ended questions avoid simple "yes/no" answers and push the celebrity to elaborate on motives, feelings, and philosophy.

  | Closed Question (Avoid)  | Open-Ended Question (DP Standard) 

  | "Was your new album hard to make?"  | "How did the emotional pressure following your last tour influence the creative process for this album?" 

  | "Do you care about what critics say?"  | "In an era where online critiques can shape an artist's career overnight, how do you protect your personal vision?" 

  | "Why did you move to London?"  | "What does working in London offer your creative process that you couldn't find back home?"

3. Model Written Interview (IB Text Type Standard)

This formatted model follows the structural, stylistic, and register conventions required for Criterion C (Writing) in DP English B.

Title: Controlling the Volume: In Conversation with Maya Lin

Byline: By Lucas Vance for Soundwave Culture Magazine

She sits back in the leather armchair of the London studio, nursing a black coffee. At twenty-four, multi-platinum producer Maya Lin looks remarkably unbothered by the media storm surrounding her latest album, Echoes. Known for her reclusive nature, Lin rarely grants sit-down interviews. Today, however, she opens up about artistic pressure, commercial success, and why silence remains her most vital instrument.

Soundwave Culture: Echoes has been streaming non-stop for two weeks, yet you spent months entirely off the grid before its release. Why the sudden vanishing act?

Maya Lin: [Laughs] It wasn’t a dramatic exit; it was survival. When you're constantly submerged in synthetic noise, you lose your internal pitch. I needed to isolate myself in northern Scotland just to remember what quiet felt like. You can’t synthesize authenticity if you aren't listening to real life.

Soundwave Culture: Some critics labeled that hiatus a risky PR stunt for a young artist at her peak.

Maya Lin: People will project whatever narrative serves their headline. To me, risk isn't stepping away from social media; risk is releasing compromise disguised as art. If I listened to every board member at the label, Echoes would just be twelve tracks of predictable radio filler.

Soundwave Culture: That uncompromising stance clearly resonated—it’s your most critically acclaimed project to date. Where do you go from here?

Maya Lin: Forward, hopefully. But on my own terms. I’ve realized success isn’t about remaining at the center of the noise; it’s about controlling the volume.

As the recording session resumes, Lin turns back to her mixing console, completely locked in. In an industry obsessed with constant visibility, her quiet conviction speaks louder than any promotional campaign ever could.
